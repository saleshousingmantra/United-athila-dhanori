How to judge an amenity list

A long list is only useful if the amenities are built, open and affordable to run. Ask three questions for every item you care about: is it ready at possession or in a later phase, who pays to run it, and how will residents book it? A swimming pool and a gymnasium need staff, cleaning and equipment, and those costs usually return as maintenance charges. A clubhouse that is free to enter but costly to maintain is not a saving.

Match the list to your household

  • Families with children: the kids' play area and landscaped gardens matter most. Check that play areas are away from roads and parking.
  • Fitness-minded buyers: look at the gym equipment, the pool's size and timings, and the jogging track's length and lighting.
  • Senior members: the senior citizen zone, seating and level paths matter more than a long list.

Visit on a weekend evening when you can see how busy shared spaces are, and ask the sales team to mark each amenity on the master plan so you can see how far it is from your tower.
